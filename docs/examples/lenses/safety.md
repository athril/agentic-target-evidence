## Summary

> The target TRPC6 shows no curated Open Targets safety liabilities and is associated with Focal Segmental Glomerulosclerosis (FSGS) through multiple genetic associations, including pathogenic variants in ClinVar. Mouse KO phenotypes include cardiovascular effects such as increased vasoconstriction and abnormal vascular smooth muscle physiology, which are candidate on-target liabilities for TRPC6 inhibition. The gene is relatively LoF-tolerant with no homozygous carriers observed in gnomAD, supporting the safety of pharmacological inhibition. Expression levels are low but uninformative in bulk kidney tissue due to podocyte-specificity, and the target is expressed at high levels in other tissues like lung and esophagus. The therapeutic direction aligns with the known gain-of-function mechanism, where TRPC6 mutations cause increased calcium influx and podocyte injury.

---

## Analysis

The safety lens assessment for TRPC6 indicates a supportive overall verdict. There are no curated Open Targets safety liabilities, and the genetic associations strongly support the target's relevance to FSGS. Mouse KO phenotypes suggest potential cardiovascular on-target effects, which must be carefully managed in clinical development. The gene is LoF-tolerant with no homozygous carriers observed, indicating that chronic pharmacological inhibition may be safe. Expression levels are low but uninformative in bulk kidney tissue due to the minor fraction of podocytes, and high in other tissues like lung and esophagus. The therapeutic direction aligns with known gain-of-function mechanisms, where TRPC6 mutations cause increased calcium influx and podocyte injury. While there is a risk of on-target effects in extra-renal tissues, the overall safety profile supports progression.
